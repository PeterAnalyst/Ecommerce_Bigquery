# Business Insights & Findings

Analyzed 869K sessions, 685K users, and $2M in revenue from the Google
Merchandise Store (2016–2017) to identify conversion inefficiencies,
revenue leakage, and growth opportunities across devices, channels,
and geographies.

---

## Key Takeaways

> The business does not have a traffic problem. It has a conversion problem.
> Fixing mobile experience alone could unlock $132K in revenue without
> acquiring a single new user.

---

## 1. Mobile Is Leaving Money on the Table

Mobile drives **28.6% of all sessions** but generates only **3.1% of
total revenue** — a conversion rate of 0.4% vs desktop's 2.0%.

This is not a traffic quality issue. The underperformance is consistent
across every acquisition channel, every geography, and both new and
returning users. The data points to one cause: **the mobile experience
is broken at the conversion step.**

| Device | Sessions | Conv Rate | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desktop | 411K | 2.0% | $990K |
| Mobile | 172K | 0.4% | $32K |
| Tablet | 25K | — | — |

**Opportunity**: If mobile conversion improved to match desktop's rate,
the business could generate an estimated **$132K in additional revenue**
without increasing traffic spend by a single dollar.

---

## 2. Retention Drives Revenue More Than Acquisition

Returning users convert at **3.71%** vs new users at **0.66%** — a
5.6× gap — yet returning users represent only 22% of total traffic.

| User Type | Sessions | Conv Rate | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Returning | 200K | 3.71% | $1.33M |
| New | 703K | 0.66% | $447K |

New users account for 78% of sessions but generate only 25% of revenue.
The current channel mix is heavily skewed toward acquisition, yet the
data clearly shows that retention delivers far higher ROI. Investing in
remarketing, loyalty programs, and post-purchase email flows would
outperform scaling new acquisition spend on low-intent traffic.

---

## 3. Channel Efficiency Varies Significantly

Google drives the majority of revenue by volume, but is not the most
efficient channel per session:

| Source | Revenue | Conv Rate | RPS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google | $169K | 1.2% | $1.36 |
| Facebook | $3K | 1.9% | $1.75 |
| Yahoo | $1K | 1.9% | $1.58 |
| Bing | $1K | 1.2% | $0.69 |

Facebook and Yahoo convert at **1.9%** — higher than Google's 1.2% —
despite a fraction of the volume. These channels are underinvested
relative to their efficiency.

On mobile specifically, Google CPC is the standout performer at **1.02%
conversion** — the only mobile channel meaningfully breaking the 1%
threshold. By contrast, YouTube mobile generates 18,309 sessions but
only $103 in total revenue (0.01% conversion), representing near-zero
commercial value.

---

## 4. High-Value Geographic Markets Are Being Overlooked

Desktop revenue per session tells a different story than raw traffic
volume:

| Country | Desktop RPS | Mobile RPS |
|---|---|---|
| Nigeria | $11.05 | — |
| Venezuela | $7.32 | — |
| United States | $6.95 | $0.54 |
| Kuwait | $2.13 | — |
| Canada | $1.49 | $0.14 |

Nigeria and Venezuela generate **desktop RPS far above the US** despite
minimal traffic investment. Maracaibo (Venezuela) also appears in the
top 20 revenue-generating cities, confirming genuine commercial demand
in that market.

Among identifiable US cities, New York ($239K), Mountain View ($143K),
and San Francisco ($109K) account for the majority of domestic revenue
and represent the highest-priority markets for localized investment.

Meanwhile, countries including France, Netherlands, Italy, Brazil,
Indonesia, Thailand, Singapore, Turkey, and Vietnam show thousands of
mobile sessions but **zero transactions** — pointing to payment method
or checkout gateway barriers rather than lack of demand.

---

## 5. Revenue Trend and Seasonality

Monthly revenue climbed steadily from January 2017 ($105K) through
April 2017 ($223K) — the peak month — before declining through summer.

| Month | Revenue | MoM Change |
|---|---|---|
| August 2016 | $189K | — |
| December 2016 | $167K | — |
| January 2017 | $105K | — |
| March 2017 | $150K | +29.9% |
| April 2017 | $223K | +48.4% |
| May 2017 | $136K | -39.1% |
| July 2017 | $160K | +25.8% |
| August 2017 | $9K | -94.5% |

Two patterns stand out:

**Q1–Q2 2017 growth is real.** Revenue more than doubled from January
to April, suggesting either a successful campaign period or seasonal
demand peak.

**August 2017 is almost certainly a data artifact.** A drop from $160K
to $9K in a single month — with no comparable decline in 2016 August
($189K) — is inconsistent with organic business behavior. This is
likely a dataset cutoff or tracking issue and should not be interpreted
as a genuine revenue collapse.

---

## 6. Overall Conclusion

The core performance issue is not traffic volume but conversion
efficiency. The business attracts healthy traffic across devices and
channels, but fails to monetize it — particularly on mobile, in
international markets, and among first-time visitors.

The three highest-leverage interventions are:

1. **Fix mobile conversion** — the single largest revenue opportunity
2. **Invest in returning users** — the most valuable and underserved segment
3. **Remove international checkout barriers** — demand exists but purchases cannot complete

## Recommendations

| Priority | Action | Expected Impact |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 High | Audit and fix mobile checkout experience | Mobile drives 28.6% of sessions but only 3.1% of revenue — closing even half the conversion gap could unlock $132K+ in additional revenue |
| 🔴 High | Clarify August 2017 revenue drop | Revenue collapsed from $160K (July) to $8.9K (August) — a 94.5% single-month drop inconsistent with all prior periods; validate before drawing any trend conclusions |
| 🔴 High | Investigate international mobile payment barriers | France, Netherlands, Italy, Brazil, Indonesia and others show thousands of mobile sessions but zero transactions — strong evidence of payment method or gateway incompatibility |
| 🟡 Medium | Invest in returning user retention | Returning users convert at 3.71% vs 0.66% for new users — a 5.6× gap — yet represent only 22% of traffic; remarketing, loyalty programs, and post-purchase flows would deliver the highest revenue ROI |
| 🟡 Medium | Scale Google CPC on mobile | At 1.02% conversion, mobile CPC is the only mobile channel meaningfully above 1%; selectively increasing paid mobile spend here could improve overall mobile monetization |
| 🟡 Medium | Scale Facebook and Yahoo paid channels | Both convert at 1.9% vs Google's 1.2% on desktop — more efficient per session and currently underinvested relative to performance |
| 🟡 Medium | Prioritize New York, Mountain View, and San Francisco | These three cities generate $491K in combined identifiable revenue; localized UX improvements or campaigns here would have disproportionate impact |
| 🟢 Low | Test Bing organic mobile | 2.46% mobile conversion rate — the highest of any mobile channel — on 122 sessions; intent quality is exceptional and worth testing at modestly higher scale |
| 🟢 Low | Cut or redirect YouTube mobile spend | 18,309 sessions generating $103 total revenue (0.01% conversion) — near-zero commercial value; budget should be redirected toward higher-intent mobile channels |
| 🟢 Low | Run geo-targeted campaigns in Nigeria and Venezuela | Nigeria leads global desktop RPS at $11.05, Maracaibo appears in top 20 revenue cities — both are high-value underinvested markets worth a targeted test |
